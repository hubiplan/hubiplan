Under **GDPR** the board keeps the documents the GDPR requires from every club that
holds member data: the record of processing activities, the list of processors and the
log of data breaches. The privacy notices for members and the website remain under
Settings › Data protection.

Record: For every enabled module HUBIplan suggests a processing activity with purpose, legal
basis, data subjects, data, recipients and erasure period. The periods match what HUBIplan
actually erases. Check the details; remove what the club does not use with **Hide**. Add what the
club processes outside HUBIplan, such as car pools or photos in a messenger group, with
**Own activity**. **Record as PDF** creates the record for filing or for the supervisory authority.

## In detail

Controller and measures: Name, address and representatives come from the legal notice. Add the
data protection officer if there is one, and who runs the server. The technical and
organisational measures are prefilled with what HUBIplan itself provides; add what the club does
beyond that.

Processors: This lists all business partners with the role **Processor** and whether a valid
**Data processing agreement** is on file. You maintain partners and agreements under
[Business partners](/geschaeftspartner); an agreement is valid until its end date.

Data breaches: **Record a data breach** documents what happened, which data and persons are
concerned, how high you rate the risk and what you did about it. From the time the breach was
discovered, there are 72 hours to report it to the supervisory authority. Open breaches appear on
the home page under **To do** until the report and closure are entered. Without a risk to the
persons concerned there is no need to report, but the breach is still documented. Closing is
only possible once the report is entered or you explain why it was not reported. **PDF** creates
the log of one breach. Description, data, consequences and measures are stored encrypted.

Permissions: Everyone with “View data protection” may read, everyone with “Edit data protection”
may maintain. Roles that can edit the settings have this permission from the start. The
suggestions are a working aid, not legal advice.
