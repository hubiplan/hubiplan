Under Design you choose the look, colours and logo of the website. The colours usually come from the unit's appearance and can be adjusted here.

When assigning colours you choose a palette colour, white or dark for each place of the design (header, menu, decorative lines, footer, colour bands, date box). The header, footer, colour bands and decorative lines can also get a gradient from the appearance settings instead of a colour; the menu, buttons and date box stay single-coloured, and rounded edges show the first colour of the gradient. Below, under **Text, surfaces and background**, you set the colour of body text, secondary text, surfaces (cards, table rows) and the page background. “Default” takes the colour ticked under **Used for** in Settings › Appearance, otherwise the design stays as it is; table rows then alternate with the surface colour; a chosen page background takes precedence over the “Background” control of the fine-tuning. If text on surfaces or background becomes hard to read (below 4.5 : 1), a notice appears.

Changes to design, colours, **Fine-tuning**, header image and home page are a draft first: visitors keep seeing the previous version. At the top you then see “Unpublished changes”. **Publish** makes the draft public, **Discard** undoes all changes. On a wide screen, a preview on the right shows the real website with the draft; it reloads after every change. At the top you choose **Phone**, **Tablet** or **Desktop**, **Reload** reconnects the preview, **In a new window** opens it full size. On narrow screens the **Preview** button opens the website in a new window. Anyone you gave a preview link can see the draft as well.

Under **Home** you build the home page from sections and see every change straight away in the preview. The existing sections (such as welcome text, upcoming events, majesties) exist once each; you switch them on and off. **Add section** adds your own sections, which you can use as often as you like: **Text**, **Text with image**, **Banner**, **Button bar**, **Tiles**, **Featured event** (with “N days to go”, disappears after the event), **Album preview**, **Quote**, **Contact persons** (only roles shown on the website, and people who have given their consent, as on the board page), **Frequently asked questions** (questions with answers to expand), **Partners and sponsors** (logos with a link; **Take over from business partners** fetches name and link of the business partners who have consented), **Directions** (address of a saved place with “Plan route”), **Events of one category** (the next public events of one category), **Document to download** (a published file from Website › Documents as a button), **Video as a link** (preview image linking to the video at the provider; the website embeds nothing), **Excerpt from the chronicle** (the latest timeline entries with a link to the chronicle), **Facts at a glance** (two to six numbers with a short text) and **Divider** (line or space). Events and chronicle only appear when their area is switched on. Drag the sections by the handle into the order you want. **Edit** opens a section's content and appearance, **Apply** saves it to the draft, **Delete** removes one of your own sections. For each section you choose the **Background** (**None**, **Light**, a palette colour or a gradient, each with a matching text colour) and the **Section width** (**Text width**, **Content width** or **Full width**). In the “Newspaper” design, **Column** sets whether a section is in the **Main column** or the **Side column**. Buttons and tiles lead to a page of the website, the contact form, an event or, as an **External link**, to a new window. People who may only “Edit content” change the content of the existing sections under Website › Home.

Under **Area display** you choose how the fixed areas look: the board as cards with photo or a compact list, events as a list by month or a monthly calendar (on a phone only the days with events appear), sub-units as large or small tiles or a list, and the gallery as tiles or masonry with pictures at their own height. A sketch shows each option. Like everything here, your choice goes into the draft first. You write an introduction at the top of an area under Website › Texts.

Under **Earlier versions** you find the last five published designs. **Restore** turns one of them into a draft; it only goes live with **Publish**.

Under **Fine-tuning** you adjust the chosen design without any programming. The settings are in six collapsible groups: font, page, cards/images/buttons, header and menu, home page and footer. Each setting has two to four fixed levels, for example corners “square”, “slight” or “round”; yes/no questions are switches. If no field is chosen, the website shows what the design provides. A second click on the chosen field resets the setting, **Reset** resets all of them. Whatever a design sets itself (such as the position of the coat of arms in the sidebar design) is greyed out. The coat of arms and the club name cannot both be hidden. The fonts are stored on the website itself (free licences); nothing is loaded from other servers.

Under **Page**, **Dark mode** › “follow device” shows the website in dark colours when the visitor's phone or computer is set to dark; header, menu and footer keep their club colours, design packages with their own CSS stay light. **Increased contrast** makes text darker and lines stronger, and the **“Back to top” button on long pages** appears at the bottom right once someone has scrolled far down. **Font** › **Size** also offers “very large”. **Image format in tiles** sets home page tiles, gallery albums and posts to 3:2, 4:3 or square. **Menu on phones** shows the menu on small screens “collapsible” behind the “Menu” button or as a **swipeable bar** below the header. Under **Home**, **Cover image** has the “medium” level like the header height, and **Image carousel advances** sets whether carousels change “only by hand”, “slowly” (each image twice as long) or “normal”.

With **Header and menu** › **Background** › **Header image** you choose an image from the image library that appears at the top of the website instead of the coat of arms and club name, for example a banner with lettering. It should contain the club name and be wide, about 1200 × 300 pixels. Without a header image, or with the background “white” or “coloured”, the coat of arms and name stay. **Header height** sets how tall the header image can get: “low”, “medium”, “normal” or “high”. “Medium” suits, for example, a wide logo with a subtitle that becomes too small at “low”. On a phone a wide header image quickly becomes too small to read next to the menu button. Below the header image you can therefore choose an optional **Header image for phones**: a more compact version, for example with the lettering on two lines. The website shows it on narrow screens below 600 pixels wide and the header image everywhere else. Without a header image for phones the header image is used everywhere.

Also under **Header and menu**, **“Home” in the menu** hides the first menu item on larger screens; the coat of arms, club name or header image then lead to the home page, and the mobile menu keeps “Home”. **Search** sets where the magnifying glass appears: “in the menu” at the end of the menu bar, “top right” in the header next to social media, or “in the footer” as a small search field. To hide the search completely, switch off the “Search” block under Website › Menu and sections › Building blocks. This keeps room in a full menu bar, for example when one item is highlighted as a button.

In the browser tab and on the phone home screen the website shows the unit's own icon, otherwise its coat of arms (square, with a transparent border). You set both under Settings › Appearance. Without an icon or coat of arms the website shows no icon of its own. Browsers often keep an old icon for a while; reloading the page helps.

Under **Design as a file**, **Save design** saves the design, colour assignment, **Fine-tuning** and **Area display** as a small file. For another unit or on another server, **Load design** loads it into the draft again; it is checked like a change made by hand. If the design does not exist there, the other values still apply. Coat of arms, texts, images and the home page are not included; the colours follow the palette of the unit that loads the file. Unlike a design package the file contains no CSS, so anyone who manages the website may load it.

Under **Share design** you save a design as a ZIP file with **Save as package**. The package contains the styling, the layout of header, footer and home page and the colour defaults, but no coat of arms, texts or images.

Platform administrators can import a package under **Load design from package**. With a new **Short name** and **Name** it becomes an own design, for example a styling variant for your club only. **Check** lets HUBIplan check the package: no JavaScript, no content from other addresses, no imported HTML/Django templates. **Import** releases the design for this website; under **Imported designs** the release can be switched on and off for each website.

Design packages use maintained built-in layouts with custom CSS and colours. Imported HTML/Django templates are rejected. Legacy packages with such templates are quarantined and display Classic until replaced by a package without templates. Layout markup follows maintained components rather than freezing historic HTML.
